# Saturn V Rocket

The Saturn V was the launch vehicle used for all crewed Apollo Moon missions as well as the Skylab space station launch. It was a three-stage, liquid-fueled rocket developed under the direction of Wernher von Braun at NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center. At the time it flew, it was the tallest, heaviest, and most powerful rocket that had been brought to operational status, standing about 111 meters (363 feet) tall.

The first stage, designated S-IC, was powered by five F-1 engines burning kerosene (RP-1) and liquid oxygen, producing about 7.5 million pounds of thrust at liftoff. The second stage, S-II, used five J-2 engines burning liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen. The third stage, S-IVB, used a single J-2 engine and was responsible for both completing the push into Earth orbit and, later, performing the Trans-Lunar Injection burn that sent the Apollo spacecraft toward the Moon.

The Saturn V flew thirteen times between 1967 and 1973, including two uncrewed test flights (Apollo 4 and Apollo 6), all the crewed Apollo missions from Apollo 8 through Apollo 17, and the launch of Skylab in 1973. It never suffered a launch failure that resulted in loss of a mission's primary payload, though Apollo 13's Saturn V experienced a premature shutdown of one second-stage engine during ascent, which the remaining engines compensated for automatically.
