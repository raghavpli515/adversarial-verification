# Apollo 17

Apollo 17 launched December 7, 1972, and returned December 19, 1972. It was the sixth and final crewed Moon landing of the Apollo program, and the last time humans have visited the Moon. It was also the only Apollo mission to launch at night.

The crew consisted of Commander Eugene "Gene" Cernan, Command Module Pilot Ronald Evans, and Lunar Module Pilot Harrison "Jack" Schmitt. Schmitt was a professional geologist, the only trained scientist among the twelve Apollo moonwalkers — all the others were test pilots or engineers by background.

The Lunar Module, nicknamed "Challenger," landed at Taurus–Littrow, a valley site chosen for its geological diversity. The Command Module was nicknamed "America." Cernan and Schmitt conducted three moonwalks using the Lunar Roving Vehicle, totaling about 22 hours on the surface — the longest lunar surface stay of the Apollo program — and collected the largest sample return of any Apollo mission, including orange-colored volcanic glass soil.

As Cernan re-entered the Lunar Module for the final time, he became the last person to walk on the Moon, remarking that the crew would "leave as we came and, God willing, as we shall return, with peace and hope for all mankind." No crewed mission has returned to the lunar surface since Apollo 17.
