# Apollo 11

Apollo 11 launched July 16, 1969, and returned July 24, 1969. It was the first mission to land humans on the Moon. The crew consisted of Commander Neil Armstrong, Command Module Pilot Michael Collins, and Lunar Module Pilot Buzz Aldrin.

The Lunar Module, nicknamed "Eagle," landed in the Sea of Tranquility (Mare Tranquillitatis) on July 20, 1969. Armstrong became the first human to step onto the lunar surface, followed about 19 minutes later by Aldrin. Collins remained in lunar orbit aboard the Command Module, nicknamed "Columbia," and did not walk on the Moon.

Armstrong's first words upon stepping onto the surface were "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." Armstrong and Aldrin spent about two and a half hours outside the Lunar Module, collecting samples, taking photographs, deploying scientific instruments, and planting a U.S. flag. They spent roughly 21.5 hours on the lunar surface in total before lifting off to rejoin Collins.

The mission fulfilled President Kennedy's 1961 goal of landing a man on the Moon and returning him safely to Earth before the end of the 1960s. The crew returned to Earth on July 24, 1969, splashing down in the Pacific Ocean and undergoing a period of quarantine as a precaution against possible lunar contamination.
