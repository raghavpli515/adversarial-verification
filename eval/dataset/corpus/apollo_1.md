# Apollo 1

Apollo 1 was the first crewed mission of the Apollo program, originally designated AS-204. It never flew. On January 27, 1967, during a launch pad test at Cape Kennedy, a fire broke out in the Command Module, killing all three crew members: Command Pilot Virgil "Gus" Grissom, Senior Pilot Ed White, and Pilot Roger Chaffee. Ed White had been the first American to walk in space, during Gemini 4 in 1965.

The fire spread rapidly in the module's pure oxygen atmosphere at sea-level pressure. The crew could not escape because the hatch opened inward and was held shut by internal cabin pressure that was higher than outside, making it impossible to open quickly under the conditions of the fire.

The mission name "Apollo 1" was adopted afterward at the request of Grissom's widow, Betty Grissom, and NASA agreed the designation would not be reused. The disaster led to a roughly 20-month grounding of crewed Apollo flights while NASA redesigned the Command Module, including replacing the inward-opening hatch with a quick-release outward-opening hatch and reducing the use of pure oxygen at high pressure during ground tests.

The next crewed Apollo mission after the fire was Apollo 7, in October 1968.
