# Apollo 13

Apollo 13 launched April 11, 1970, and returned April 17, 1970. It was intended to be the third crewed Moon landing, targeting the Fra Mauro highlands, but the landing was aborted after an in-flight emergency. It is often described as a "successful failure" because the crew survived a life-threatening malfunction and returned safely.

The crew consisted of Commander James "Jim" Lovell, Command Module Pilot John "Jack" Swigert, and Lunar Module Pilot Fred Haise. Swigert was a late replacement for Ken Mattingly, who was removed from the crew days before launch after exposure to German measles (rubella), even though Mattingly never developed the illness.

About 56 hours into the mission, roughly 330,000 kilometers from Earth, an oxygen tank in the Service Module exploded, crippling the Command Module's power, water, and oxygen supplies and forcing the crew to abandon the Moon landing. Swigert's report to Mission Control, commonly paraphrased as "Houston, we've had a problem," began the crisis.

The crew used the Lunar Module, nicknamed "Aquarius," as a "lifeboat" for consumables and propulsion while the Command Module, nicknamed "Odyssey," was powered down and preserved for reentry. Mission Control and the crew improvised procedures, including a makeshift carbon dioxide scrubber adapter, to keep the crew alive on a free-return trajectory around the Moon and back to Earth. All three astronauts returned safely, splashing down in the Pacific Ocean on April 17, 1970. The Fra Mauro landing site was later visited by Apollo 14.
