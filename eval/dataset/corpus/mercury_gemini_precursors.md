# Mercury and Gemini: Precursor Programs

Before Apollo, NASA ran two crewed spaceflight programs that developed the techniques Apollo would depend on: Project Mercury and Project Gemini. Both are frequently confused with Apollo missions in casual references, but they used different spacecraft, different astronauts in different roles, and never involved a Moon landing attempt.

Project Mercury (1958–1963) flew single-astronaut capsules. Alan Shepard became the first American in space with a suborbital flight in May 1961, shortly after Soviet cosmonaut Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space. John Glenn became the first American to orbit the Earth in February 1962. Mercury proved that humans could survive and function in the space environment.

Project Gemini (1965–1966) flew two-astronaut spacecraft and was designed specifically to develop techniques required for a Moon mission: orbital rendezvous and docking, extended-duration flight, and extravehicular activity (spacewalks). Ed White performed the first American spacewalk on Gemini 4 in 1965; he later died in the Apollo 1 fire. Neil Armstrong commanded Gemini 8 in 1966, which performed the first docking of two spacecraft in orbit, though the mission was cut short after a thruster malfunction caused dangerous spacecraft spin. Jim Lovell and Buzz Aldrin also flew Gemini missions before their later Apollo flights.

Several astronauts who flew Mercury or Gemini missions went on to fly Apollo missions, but the reverse — an astronaut whose first spaceflight was on Apollo — was also common among the later Apollo crews, since NASA continued recruiting new astronaut groups throughout the 1960s.
