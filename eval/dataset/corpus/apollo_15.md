# Apollo 15

Apollo 15 launched July 26, 1971, and returned August 7, 1971. It was the fourth crewed Moon landing and the first of the three "J missions" — extended-duration missions with a greater focus on scientific exploration, using an upgraded Lunar Module capable of longer surface stays and heavier equipment.

The crew consisted of Commander David Scott, Command Module Pilot Alfred Worden, and Lunar Module Pilot James Irwin. The Lunar Module, nicknamed "Falcon," landed at Hadley–Apennine, a site near a lunar rille and the Apennine mountain range chosen for its geological interest. The Command Module was nicknamed "Endeavour."

Apollo 15 was the first mission to carry the Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV), a battery-powered car that allowed astronauts to travel several kilometers from the Lunar Module, greatly extending the area they could explore compared to earlier missions where astronauts explored on foot. Scott and Irwin conducted three moonwalks using the rover, totaling about 18.5 hours on the surface, and collected the "Genesis Rock," a sample of ancient lunar crust.

Worden performed a deep-space spacewalk during the return trip to retrieve film canisters from the Service Module's exterior, one of the few spacewalks conducted in deep space (as opposed to Earth or lunar orbit) during the 20th century.
