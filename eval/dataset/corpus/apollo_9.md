# Apollo 9

Apollo 9 launched March 3, 1969, and returned March 13, 1969. It was an Earth-orbit mission that carried out the first crewed test flight of the full Apollo spacecraft stack, including the Lunar Module (LM), which had not flown with a crew before.

The crew consisted of Commander James McDivitt, Command Module Pilot David Scott, and Lunar Module Pilot Russell "Rusty" Schweickart. The Command/Service Module was nicknamed "Gumdrop" and the Lunar Module was nicknamed "Spider" — the first mission on which the two spacecraft were given individual call-sign names, since for the first time in the program they needed to be referred to separately after undocking.

During the mission, McDivitt and Schweickart flew the Lunar Module independently of the Command Module in Earth orbit, testing its engines and practicing the rendezvous and docking maneuvers that would be required near the Moon. Schweickart also performed a spacewalk to test the Apollo spacesuit's self-contained life support system, which would be used for lunar surface exploration.

Apollo 9 validated that the Lunar Module could operate, maneuver, and redock successfully, clearing a critical milestone before a lunar-orbit test of the same hardware could be attempted on Apollo 10.
