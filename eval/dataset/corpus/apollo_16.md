# Apollo 16

Apollo 16 launched April 16, 1972, and returned April 27, 1972. It was the fifth crewed Moon landing and the second of the three "J missions." It targeted the Descartes Highlands, a lunar highland region chosen because scientists expected, incorrectly as it turned out, to find volcanic terrain there.

The crew consisted of Commander John Young, Command Module Pilot Thomas "Ken" Mattingly, and Lunar Module Pilot Charles Duke. Mattingly, who had been removed from the Apollo 13 crew due to measles exposure, finally flew to the Moon on this mission. Young had previously flown on Apollo 10 and would later command Space Shuttle missions.

The Lunar Module, nicknamed "Orion," landed in the Descartes Highlands. The Command Module was nicknamed "Casper." Young and Duke conducted three moonwalks using the Lunar Roving Vehicle, totaling about 20 hours on the surface, and drove faster and farther than on Apollo 15.

Geological analysis of the samples later showed the Descartes Highlands were formed by impact processes rather than volcanic activity, a scientifically valuable finding even though it contradicted the mission's original hypothesis.
