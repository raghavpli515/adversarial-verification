# Apollo 10

Apollo 10 launched May 18, 1969, and returned May 26, 1969. It served as a full "dress rehearsal" for the first Moon landing, testing every phase of a landing mission except the landing itself.

The crew consisted of Commander Thomas Stafford, Command Module Pilot John Young, and Lunar Module Pilot Eugene Cernan. The Command/Service Module was nicknamed "Charlie Brown" and the Lunar Module was nicknamed "Snoopy," after the Peanuts comic strip characters, which NASA had adopted as informal mascots for flight safety.

Stafford and Cernan flew the Lunar Module down to within about 15.6 kilometers (8.4 nautical miles) of the lunar surface before firing its ascent engine to rejoin Young in the Command Module in lunar orbit — deliberately stopping short of an actual landing, partly because this particular Lunar Module was too heavy to lift back off the Moon safely.

Apollo 10 confirmed that the combined hardware and procedures worked end-to-end in lunar orbit, giving NASA confidence to attempt an actual landing on the very next mission, Apollo 11, two months later.
