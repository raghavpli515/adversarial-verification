# Neil Armstrong

Neil Alden Armstrong (August 5, 1930 – August 25, 2012) was an American astronaut and the first person to walk on the Moon, as commander of Apollo 11 in July 1969. Before joining NASA's astronaut corps, he served as a naval aviator during the Korean War and later worked as a test pilot, flying the X-15 rocket plane for the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA), NASA's predecessor agency.

Armstrong's first spaceflight was Gemini 8 in March 1966, which he commanded. During that mission, he and pilot David Scott performed the first successful docking of two spacecraft in orbit, but a stuck thruster caused the combined spacecraft to spin dangerously, and Armstrong had to use manual control to stabilize it and perform an early, safe re-entry.

On Apollo 11, Armstrong served as commander and was the first of the crew to step onto the lunar surface on July 20, 1969, followed by Buzz Aldrin about 19 minutes later. His words upon stepping onto the surface, "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind," are among the most quoted lines in spaceflight history.

After Apollo 11, Armstrong largely retreated from public life, taught aerospace engineering at the University of Cincinnati, and rarely gave interviews. He served on the investigation panel for the 1970 Apollo 13 accident and, later in life, the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster in 1986. He never flew in space again after Apollo 11.
