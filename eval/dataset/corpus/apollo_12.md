# Apollo 12

Apollo 12 launched November 14, 1969, and returned November 24, 1969. It was the second crewed Moon landing. The crew consisted of Commander Charles "Pete" Conrad, Command Module Pilot Richard Gordon, and Lunar Module Pilot Alan Bean.

The launch vehicle was struck by lightning twice within the first minute after liftoff, briefly disrupting the spacecraft's electrical systems, but the crew and ground controllers recovered the systems and the mission continued as planned.

The Lunar Module, nicknamed "Intrepid," landed in the Ocean of Storms (Oceanus Procellarum). Unlike Apollo 11, which landed with some distance from its original target, Apollo 12 demonstrated a precision landing, touching down within walking distance of the uncrewed Surveyor 3 probe, which had landed there in 1967. Conrad and Bean retrieved parts of Surveyor 3 to bring back to Earth for study. Gordon remained in orbit aboard the Command Module, nicknamed "Yankee Clipper."

Conrad and Bean conducted two moonwalks totaling about 7 hours 45 minutes on the surface. Apollo 12 confirmed that pinpoint lunar landings were possible, which was essential for later missions targeting scientifically interesting but harder-to-reach sites.
