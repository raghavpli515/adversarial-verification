# Buzz Aldrin

Edwin Eugene "Buzz" Aldrin Jr. (born January 20, 1930) was the Lunar Module Pilot on Apollo 11 and the second person to walk on the Moon, following Neil Armstrong onto the surface about 19 minutes after him on July 20, 1969. Unlike Armstrong and Collins, whose backgrounds were primarily in test piloting, Aldrin held a doctorate in astronautics from MIT, where his 1963 thesis focused on orbital rendezvous techniques — directly relevant to the docking maneuvers Apollo would later depend on.

Aldrin's first spaceflight was Gemini 12 in November 1966, the final mission of the Gemini program. On that flight he performed a then-record-setting spacewalk of about 5.5 hours, demonstrating that astronauts could work effectively outside the spacecraft after earlier Gemini spacewalks had proven more physically exhausting than expected.

On Apollo 11, Aldrin operated the Lunar Module's systems during the powered descent to the surface and, once on the Moon, took the well-known photographs of Armstrong (Armstrong took most of the surface photographs of Aldrin himself, meaning most iconic Apollo 11 surface photos show Aldrin rather than Armstrong). Michael Collins remained in lunar orbit throughout the surface excursion and did not walk on the Moon.

After leaving NASA, Aldrin became a public advocate for continued space exploration, including future crewed missions to Mars, and wrote several books about his experiences and his views on the future of spaceflight.
