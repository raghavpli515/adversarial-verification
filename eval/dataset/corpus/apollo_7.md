# Apollo 7

Apollo 7 was the first crewed flight of the Apollo program, launched October 11, 1968, and returning October 22, 1968. It was an eleven-day Earth-orbit mission designed to test the Command/Service Module (CSM) with a crew aboard; no Lunar Module was carried on this flight.

The crew consisted of Commander Walter "Wally" Schirra, Command Module Pilot Donn Eisele, and Lunar Module Pilot Walter Cunningham. Despite the mission title "Lunar Module Pilot," no Lunar Module flew on Apollo 7. This was Schirra's third spaceflight (after Mercury and Gemini missions) and the only spaceflight for both Eisele and Cunningham.

Apollo 7 was the first three-person American space mission and the first American spaceflight to broadcast live television from orbit. The mission successfully validated the CSM's systems for the crewed missions that would follow, clearing the way for Apollo 8 to attempt a lunar orbit mission just two months later.

The mission was generally considered a technical success, though it was also known for tension between the crew and mission control, partly due to the astronauts developing head colds during the flight.
