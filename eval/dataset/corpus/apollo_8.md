# Apollo 8

Apollo 8 launched December 21, 1968, and returned December 27, 1968. It was the first crewed spacecraft to leave low Earth orbit, travel to the Moon, orbit it, and return — a major and risky acceleration of the original mission plan, undertaken partly in response to intelligence suggesting the Soviet Union might attempt a crewed lunar flyby first.

The crew consisted of Commander Frank Borman, Command Module Pilot James "Jim" Lovell, and Lunar Module Pilot William "Bill" Anders. No Lunar Module was carried on this flight; the "Lunar Module Pilot" title referred to the crew role, not equipment carried. The spacecraft orbited the Moon ten times over about 20 hours.

Apollo 8 is well known for two things: the "Earthrise" photograph of the Earth rising over the lunar horizon, taken by Bill Anders, and a live television broadcast on Christmas Eve 1968 in which the crew took turns reading the first ten verses of the Book of Genesis.

Jim Lovell later commanded Apollo 13, meaning he flew to the Moon twice but never walked on its surface — Apollo 8 orbited without landing, and Apollo 13's landing was aborted after an in-flight explosion.
