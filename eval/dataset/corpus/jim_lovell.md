# Jim Lovell

James "Jim" Lovell Jr. (born March 25, 1928) was one of the most experienced astronauts of the Gemini and Apollo eras, flying four spaceflights in total: Gemini 7 (1965), Gemini 12 (1966), Apollo 8 (1968), and Apollo 13 (1970). He is notable for having traveled to the Moon twice without ever walking on its surface.

On Apollo 8 in December 1968, Lovell served as Command Module Pilot under Commander Frank Borman, on the first crewed mission to orbit the Moon. The mission did not carry a Lunar Module and did not attempt a landing.

On Apollo 13 in April 1970, Lovell served as mission commander. The mission's planned landing at the Fra Mauro highlands was aborted after an oxygen tank explosion crippled the Service Module roughly 56 hours into the flight. Lovell and his crewmates, Jack Swigert and Fred Haise, used the Lunar Module as a lifeboat and returned safely to Earth without landing on the Moon. Lovell therefore never walked on the lunar surface despite flying to the Moon on two separate missions.

Lovell retired from NASA and the Navy in 1973. He later co-wrote the book "Lost Moon" about the Apollo 13 mission, which was adapted into the 1995 film "Apollo 13," in which he was portrayed by actor Tom Hanks.
