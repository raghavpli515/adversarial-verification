# Apollo 14

Apollo 14 launched January 31, 1971, and returned February 9, 1971. It was the third crewed Moon landing, and the first mission to land after the Apollo 13 emergency. It targeted the Fra Mauro highlands, the site originally intended for Apollo 13.

The crew consisted of Commander Alan Shepard, Command Module Pilot Stuart Roosa, and Lunar Module Pilot Edgar Mitchell. Shepard had been the first American in space, on a suborbital Mercury flight in May 1961, nearly a decade before this mission. At age 47, he was the oldest of the twelve astronauts who walked on the Moon during the Apollo program.

The Lunar Module, nicknamed "Antares," landed in the Fra Mauro highlands. Roosa remained in orbit aboard the Command Module, nicknamed "Kitty Hawk." Shepard and Mitchell conducted two moonwalks, including a long traverse toward Cone Crater. Shepard is famous for hitting two golf balls with a makeshift club during the mission's second moonwalk, a widely remembered moment of the Apollo program.

Apollo 14 demonstrated that NASA could recover from the Apollo 13 failure and resume successful lunar landings, and it completed the scientific objectives originally planned for Apollo 13.
