# Apollo 18, 19, and 20 (Cancelled Missions)

Apollo 18, Apollo 19, and Apollo 20 were planned Moon landing missions that were cancelled before launch and never flew. No crews were permanently assigned, no launch dates were finalized, and no astronauts walked on the Moon under these mission numbers.

NASA announced the cancellation of Apollo 20 in January 1970, primarily to free up a Saturn V rocket for the planned Skylab space station program, and cancelled Apollo 18 and Apollo 19 in September 1970, citing federal budget cuts to NASA's post-Apollo funding. Public and congressional interest in continuing expensive Moon missions had also declined after Apollo 11's goal was achieved.

Tentative landing sites had been studied for these missions, including areas near the crater Copernicus and the Marius Hills, but no site was ever formally finalized for a specific cancelled mission, and any specific claim about where Apollo 18, 19, or 20 "landed" or what their crews "found" is incorrect — none of them flew.

Some flight hardware built for the cancelled missions, including Saturn V rockets and Command/Service Modules, was repurposed. One Saturn V was used to launch the Skylab space station in 1973. A Command/Service Module originally built for the Apollo program was used for the 1975 Apollo–Soyuz Test Project, a joint low Earth orbit docking mission with the Soviet Union, which was the final flight of Apollo-era hardware.
